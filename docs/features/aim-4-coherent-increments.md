# Coherent increments and bounded autonomy

PO owns Epic intent, scope, quality and product priority. TDO creates and owns
User Stories and their criteria, then commits to a coherent increment whose
combined value is explicit. This updates the previous PO-owned Story model;
historical requirements and acceptance records keep their original meaning.

TDO selects implementation order from dependencies and integration risk within
the available mandate. Parts are verified during development; the delivery has
one integrated review and validation cycle with coverage for every selected
criterion. Session boundaries do not reduce the commitment. Story Points and
automatic complexity-based changes to priority, cost profile or agent count
are not part of this model.

Routine implementation, bounded uncertainty checks and correction of material
quality defects proceed within authority. Additional deliverables, experiments
and cumulative resource expansion need applicable authority before investment.
Reusable instructions live in existing contracts, not a new approval process.

Product review checks the actual integrated result and useful, accurate copy.
Headings and explanatory text must add information; redundant slogans and
decorative heading stacks should be removed while preserving accessibility.
Personality and expressive writing remain appropriate when they serve the product.

## Runtime and verification boundaries

The existing product ledger already freezes selected requirements and plan
evidence, permits only one active delivery and requires complete criterion
evidence before user acceptance. These protections are retained. UI handoffs
now assign Story formulation to TDO and Epic intent and product priority to PO.
The helpers do not authenticate role identity, judge aesthetic quality or meter
total agent expenditure. Fingerprints establish freshness, not product quality.

Approved-scope replanning is not supported by the Story CLI. Block the affected
delivery with remaining commitments and a recommendation; preserve its scope.
Rework corrects implementation under that scope, and cancel applies only to an
unapproved plan. Do not bypass these boundaries by editing the ledger directly.

AIM UI opens on the coherent increment, displaying its title and goal, selected
Stories, criterion evidence and next action. Plan approval and user acceptance
are separate facts. Recorded evidence counts do not imply completion, passing
checks or acceptance. Frozen scope order is preserved; TDO still chooses the
actual execution order. Epic criteria are inspectable separately, without
claiming Story evidence proves whole-Epic closure. No runtime migration is needed.
Planning cannot replace an active commitment or suggest cancelling approved
scope. Role cards describe responsibility, not agent presence. Current and legacy
navigation retain their distinct meanings. Prompt buttons remain discussions,
not direct approval or acceptance controls.

Focused UI tests exercise emitted instructions, evidence keyed by both Story and
criterion, preserved scope order, rework evidence removal, blocked acceptance and
authority boundaries. Browser checks use clearly synthetic lifecycle fixtures.
Existing Story lifecycle, packaged CLI, snapshot, acceptance, recovery and
delegation tests cover the retained runtime guarantees. These checks do not
establish that this revision produces better end products; that needs the
separately authorized product comparison.
